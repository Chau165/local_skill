# Visual style handoff

Create `visual-style-brief.md` only when the request asks for reusable visual or animation design grammar.

## Required sections

1. Illustration mode: flat cartoon, cel shading, hand-drawn, cutout, painted, vector-like, raster, or mixed; separate observation from inference about production technique.
2. Palette and rendering: color families, outline hierarchy, texture, shading, contrast, and material cues. Transfer relationships, not exact proprietary swatches.
3. Depth system: background, midground, foreground, atmosphere, occluders, and whether parallax is observed, selective, or unknown.
4. Asset construction: character proportions and rig candidates; object, building, vehicle, machine, and environment requirements.
5. Composition: focal hierarchy, negative space, subject scale, mobile readability, and detail density.
6. Motion grammar: camera, subject, environment, interaction, transition, static hold, and local-motion behavior. Never call a global zoom environment animation.
7. Text system: subtitle placement, fact labels, safe areas, and whether text is baked or separate when this can be observed.
8. Topic adaptation: explain how a stable style bible can generate new subject-specific worlds without carrying the reference's nouns into unrelated topics.
9. Production handoff: renderer-neutral requirements for `STYLE_BIBLE.md`, a topic-to-world asset matrix, `ASSET_PLAN.md`, `asset-manifest.json`, a contact sheet, and independently animatable local files.
10. Originality and unknowns: exclude protected identities, assets, scripts, voices, compositions, and unverified software or provenance claims.

## Evidence rules

- Cite evidence IDs in every observed or inferred subsection.
- Use timestamp ranges for motion claims and page or frame locators for static design claims.
- Record the source's actual continuity model. If it uses multiple shots or locations, do not describe it as one world merely because the art direction is consistent.
- Treat software, rigging method, source asset format, retention, and revenue as unknown unless direct evidence establishes them.

## Adaptation rule

Write recommendations as roles and relationships: guide, actor, target, prop, environment, reaction, camera handoff, and depth layer. Do not prescribe the reference's mascot, character proportions element-for-element, exact scenes, exact palette, or shot order.
